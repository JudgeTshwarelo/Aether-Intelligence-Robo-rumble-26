# AETHER INTELLIGENT EMERGENCY RESPONSE ROVER — 3D-PRINTABLE BODY SHELL

## 1. Project objective

Generate a detailed, dimensionally accurate, **3D-printable STL model of the external body shell only** for the AETHER Intelligent Emergency Response Rover. Use the provided reference images: the first image shows my actual four-wheel robotic car chassis, and the second image shows the futuristic AETHER rover design that I want to reproduce.

The final design must combine the futuristic, angular, armored appearance of the AETHER medical rover with the compact dimensions and four-wheel layout of my actual chassis.

This is a functional engineering component, not merely a visual concept, decorative model, or non-printable rendering.

## 2. Overall exterior design

Create a compact, futuristic, autonomous emergency-response rover with the following features:

- A low-profile, angular, armored exterior inspired by the AETHER rover reference image.
- A sleek, wedge-shaped front with sloped surfaces and a slightly raised central roof.
- A geometric, faceted body with clean edges, realistic proportions, and smooth transitions between panels.
- A professional industrial design suitable for both police operations and emergency medical response.
- A predominantly white or light-grey exterior with dark recessed details represented by the geometry where appropriate.
- A compact roof section for mounting sensors and communication equipment.
- A visible front sensor housing integrated into the body.
- Side panels that cover and protect the electronics.
- A rear section with sufficient space for wiring, connectors, and access to the chassis electronics.
- Exposed wheels and wheel openings that preserve the mobility of the original four-wheel chassis.

The exterior should communicate the same design language as the reference image: advanced robotics, rugged construction, intelligent emergency response, and professional engineering.

Do not reproduce the six-wheel arrangement in the reference image. My actual chassis has four wheels, and the body must be designed around that configuration.

## 7. Hollow internal electronics compartment

Create a spacious hollow interior that conceals and protects the existing electronics while allowing access for repairs and upgrades.

The internal compartment should accommodate the actual components installed on the chassis, including the microcontroller, motor driver, power distribution, wiring, connectors, and camera interface electronics where applicable.

Required structural features:

- A hollow underside that fits over the chassis.
- An internal roof clearance suitable for the tallest component.
- Reinforced internal mounting bosses.
- Cable-routing channels and rounded cable pass-through holes.
- Adequate spacing around connectors and removable circuit boards.
- A rear or upper removable access panel secured with screws.
- Rounded internal corners where possible to reduce stress concentrations.
- Ventilation openings if required by the electronics and enclosure design.
- Protection against accidental contact with moving wheels and exposed electrical connections.

Do not create a solid block of plastic. The main body must be hollow and usable as an electronics enclosure.

Do not permanently seal the underside. The shell must be removable so the chassis, batteries, motors, and electronics can be serviced.

Do not assume the shell is waterproof simply because it looks armored. Any sealing features must be designed and tested separately.

## 10. Mechanical dimensions and 3D-printing constraints

Design the body as a practical FDM-printed enclosure.

Use the following starting parameters, subject to chassis measurements and printer capability:

- Main wall thickness: approximately 2.0–2.5 mm.
- Reinforced mounting areas: approximately 3.0–4.0 mm thick.
- Screw bosses: sized for the selected screws and printer tolerances.
- General clearance for fitted parts: approximately 0.3–0.5 mm per side, adjusted for the actual printer and fit.
- Internal edges: rounded where practical.
- External panel thickness: sufficient to prevent fragile, unsupported sections.
- Split the body into printable sections if the complete shell exceeds the printer's build volume.

Use dimensions in millimetres.

Design the body so it can be printed in a sensible orientation with minimal supports. Where necessary, split the model into a main shell, removable upper cover, and separate sensor brackets.

Make all mounting holes, sensor apertures, wheel cutouts, and access-panel interfaces real geometric features.

Ensure the exported meshes are watertight, manifold, correctly oriented, and suitable for slicing.

Avoid intersecting surfaces, non-manifold edges, internal faces that confuse the slicer, zero-thickness walls, and unsupported geometry.

- The STL units and overall dimensions are correct.

The final result should resemble the futuristic AETHER emergency-response rover in the reference image, but it must be a compact, practical, four-wheel, chassis-compatible body that can actually be 3D-printed, assembled, and tested.
